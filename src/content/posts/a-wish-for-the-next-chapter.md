A project page usually explains what a product does. We also leave a little space for what someone hopes it might become.

That hope can be specific: helping people work with more clarity, making a difficult idea easier to understand, or giving a small team the confidence to keep going. It gives a visitor something human to notice alongside the features.

## Give the maker's words their own place

When a maker shares a project, they can include a wish for its next chapter. Those words belong to the person who supplied them. A project discovered through editorial research has a different origin, and its listing should say so.

The distinction lets you understand whose voice you are reading. We can gather a project under this sky and write an editorial blessing without speaking on behalf of its maker.

## Let encouragement be thoughtful

The blessings on [Premortem](/tool/premortem), [Halv](/tool/halv) and [Octop](/tool/octop) accompany their descriptions. A blessing can hope for clearer questions, calmer work or useful connections. It does not establish a product's quality or guarantee an outcome.

You still have the practical information close by: the project's own website, its availability, pricing information where it is known, and sources you can inspect.

## A small light is enough

Lighting a star is a small gesture of encouragement. The displayed count represents browser encouragement; it is not a count of people or a product rating. A bookmark can help you return to a project, while a visit to the official site gives you a way to explore further.

If you are building something, [share your project and your wish](/submit). A clear description tells people where you are today. A few honest words about your hopes can give them a reason to follow the next chapter.
